Bug: Activating a one-shot layer (e.g. `c+space = oneshot(macros)`) and then pressing a key mapped to `oneshotm` (e.g. `dot = oneshotm(shift, macro(dot space))`) executed the macro and armed `shift`, but left `macros` active indefinitely (`oneshot_depth == 1`). A subsequent keypress (e.g. `a`) resolved against the lingering `macros` layer (`a = oneshotm(shift, macro(S-1 space))`, producing `! `) rather than falling back to `[main]` with shift (`A`).

Design Decision & Fix:
Rather than unconditionally clearing all active one-shot layers on every `Op::Oneshot*` action (which would break legitimate sequential modifier chaining like `oneshot(shift)` + `oneshot(control)` on `[main]`), `Op::Oneshot`, `Op::OneshotM`, `Op::OneshotK`, and `Op::OneshotMulti` check the originating layer index (`layer` parameter in `execute_descriptor`). If `layer` is a valid layer and `self.layer_state[layer].oneshot_depth > 0`, the originating layer is consumed (`deactivate_layer(layer)` and `oneshot_depth -= 1`) before activating the target one-shot layer(s).

Tests:
`test_oneshotm_inside_oneshot_layer_pops_originating_layer` and `test_oneshot_modifier_chaining_aggregates` in `src/test_io.rs`.
